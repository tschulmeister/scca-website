II. Special Community Benefits District (SCBD) Presentation

President Chris Eichenlaub presented a PowerPoint outlining the proposed Special Community Benefits District (SCBD). The presentation contained approximately 10 slides. Related information was also available on the SCCA website and in the newsletter mailed to residents.

1. SCCA Organization and Governing Documents

  • The presentation addressed SCCA’s organizational status and governing documents.
  • SCCA is a nonprofit community association. The Board reported obtaining a legal opinion supporting its position that SCCA is not subject to Maryland Title 11B, the Maryland Homeowners Association Act.
  • The Board holds a 2026 Certificate of Good Standing from the Maryland Department of Assessments and Taxation.
  • Section 1 covenants were renewed in 2017; Section 2 covenants have expired.
  • The 1980 Articles of Incorporation and 1980 SCCA Bylaws apply to both Sections 1 and 2.
  • The rumor Anne Arundel County would take over SCCA is without merit. The SCCA Board of Directors is fully staffed, and there is no chance the County will take over SCCA.

2. Communication with Residents

  • The Board has updated the SCCA website, which includes a discussion forum. The Board is also improving email communications and plans to hold quarterly virtual meetings.
  • Residents were encouraged to provide their email addresses to receive community news and remain informed.

3. Voluntary Dues and Community Expenses

  • SCCA has 283 homes in Sections 1 and 2. In 2025, approximately 68% of households paid the annual voluntary dues of $75.
  • Approximately $17,500 in dues revenue was collected, reducing the funds available to cover rising expenses, including tree removal and insurance.

4. Existing Special Community Benefits Districts

  • The presentation noted that SCBDs are common in Anne Arundel County and cited Chartwell as an example.

5. Board Decision to Pursue an SCBD

  • In December 2025, the Board voted to pursue establishing an SCBD in response to declining collections of voluntary dues.
  • In July 2026, the Anne Arundel County legal office affirmed SCCA’s eligibility to pursue SCBD formation.

6. Proposed County Administration

Under the proposed arrangement, Anne Arundel County would:

  • Collect the SCCA assessment through property tax bills.
  • Charge a 5% administrative fee for collection.
  • Disburse collected assessments to SCCA in several allocations.

The presentation explained that establishing an SCBD would require signatures from two-thirds of the homeowners listed on the property deeds. Names on petitions must match Anne Arundel County records.

7. Steps to Establish an SCBD

The presentation outlined the following process if the required petition signatures are obtained:

  1. The Board submits the petitions to the County’s SCBD Coordinator for verification.
  2. The SCBD Coordinator counts and verifies the signatures.
  3. The SCBD Coordinator prepares a legislative request to establish the district.
  4. The County Executive forwards the proposed legislation to the County Council.
  5. The County Auditor reviews the legislation and submits findings to the County Council.
  6. The County Council advertises the proposed legislation in a local newspaper and conducts a public hearing in conjunction with a regular Council meeting.
  7. The County Council votes on the legislation. If approved, the County Executive signs it.
  8. The SCBD becomes law 45 days after the County Executive signs the legislation.

8. Proposed Uses of SCBD Funds and Anticipated Costs

The presentation described the proposed uses of SCBD funds, subject to applicable requirements:

  • Maintaining and improving community-owned real and personal property, including open space, recreational property, and entrance signs.
  • Paying eligible expenses necessary to accomplish these purposes, including loan repayment and interest, insurance, legal fees, utilities, and permitted administrative expenses.

The presentation indicated that SCBD funds could not be used for certain general administrative expenses, such as website and newsletter costs. SCCA will continue collecting nominal annual dues to cover administrative expenses not eligible for SCBD funding.

For the first year, the Board anticipated the following annual charges:

  • SCBD assessment: $65 per household.
  • SCCA dues: $10 per household for administrative expenses not covered by the SCBD.
  • The proposed combined annual amount would be $75 per household, subject to the applicable approval process.

III. Questions and Answers

Residents raised questions and comments regarding the proposed SCBD, community communications, expenses, and maintenance responsibilities.

  1. How are petition signatures counted?
    Response: Each person on the deed must sign the petition for it to be counted.
  2. A resident expressed the need for better communication.
    Response: The Board has updated the website, improved email communications, and plans to hold quarterly virtual meetings.
  3. A resident stated that the voluntary nature of the dues was a factor in purchasing their home and expressed concern about mandatory payments.
    Response: If two-thirds of homeowners sign the petition, and the County approves the SCBD, the assessment would become mandatory under the approved arrangement.
  4. Can the community leave the SCBD if residents are dissatisfied with it?
    Response: Termination of the SCBD would require a petition signed by two-thirds of the homeowners and County approval.
  5. Can the assessment be capped?
    Response: There is no cap and residents would have opportunities to provide input on the budget through quarterly meetings.
  6. What additional costs should residents consider, including advertising and periodic audits?
    A resident recommended obtaining the County’s SCBD information pamphlet to ensure that all potential expenses are considered. These included advertising the proposed legislation at least once a week for four weeks in at least one newspaper of general circulation in the County and the cost of an audit every four years.
    Response: Advertising was described as a one-time startup expense, while audit expenses would recur every four years.
  7. Has the Board compared the costs of tree service and insurance providers?
    Response: Vice President Jim Guilfoyle explained that the Board believes SCCA receives a favorable price from its current tree service provider and can negotiate service pricing. He also noted that insurance costs had not been shopped among providers recently.
  8. What happens when trees fall onto common ground?
    Response: The Vice President explained that trees that fall in SCCA common areas are not removed.
  9. Will a $65 SCBD assessment be sufficient to cover expenses?
    Response: The Board anticipates that the $65 annual SCBD assessment, together with $10 in annual SCCA dues for administrative expenses not covered by the SCBD, would cover anticipated expenses.
  10. A resident expressed confidence in the Board, stating, “I trust you with my 20 cents a day ($75/365).”
    Response: The Board thanked the resident for expressing trust.
