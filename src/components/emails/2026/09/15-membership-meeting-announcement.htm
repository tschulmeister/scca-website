Key Agenda & Bylaw Highlights

­

As outlined in Articles V & VI of our Bylaws, the Annual Meeting is the most significant democratic forum of our fiscal year. Key business items for this year's meeting include:

­
  • Financial Review
    • The treasurer’s financial report and findings from the Special Auditing Committee
  • SCBD Updates
    • Status report and discussion under regarding our Special Community Benefit District petition. 
  • Community Open Floor
    • This is an opportunity for residents to introduce new business and share feedback.
­
  • Election
    • We currently have three openings on the Board of Directors: Treasurer and two Director positions.
  • Voting & Quorum
    • Per Article V, Section 4, each household in good standing is entitled to two (2) voting privileges.
    • Reaching a 25% voting quorum (via in-person attendance or absentee ballots) is essential to validate our elections.
    • Official election packages will be distributed by October 6, 2026.

Important Governance & Community Clarifications

  1. Private vs. Official SCCA Meetings
    The SCCA Board is aware of independent flyers circulating regarding an SCBD discussion meeting on September 17th. Please note this is a private discussion group meeting and not an official SCCA Board event. Verified facts, official timelines, and accurate documentation are available anytime on the official SCCA website.
  2. Legal Notice Regarding Mailbox Flyer Distribution
    After receiving questions from residents we wanted to clarify and make everyone aware that under federal law (18 U.S.C. § 1725), placing unstamped mailable matter into a mail receptacle is illegal and subject to postal enforcement. Mailboxes are restricted solely to official U.S. Postal Service deliveries. If you or your group wish to distribute informational flyers, please use non-mailbox delivery methods such as door hangers, or contact the board to utilize official digital communication channels.
  3. Association Governance & Receivership Clarification
    Questions were recently raised regarding county intervention or "receivership" in the event of vacant Board positions following an Annual Meeting. Because SCCA is a voluntary civic association, statutory receivership mechanisms for mandatory HOAs do not apply under Maryland law. Furthermore, under Article VI, Section 3 of our Bylaws, the sitting Board retains full legal authority to fill vacant seats to ensure continuous, stable operations.
