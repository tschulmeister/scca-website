Here is a structured, data-backed synthesis of the multi-year financial trends for the **Shipley's Choice Community Association (SCCA)**, translated into key arguments for declaring the community a **Special Community Benefits District (SCBD)** under [Anne Arundel County SCBD Guidelines](https://www.aacounty.org/sites/default/files/2024-10/scbd-guidelines.pdf).

---

## 1. The Voluntary Dues Model Is Systemically Eroding

* **Loss of Paying Households:** Participation in voluntary dues has steadily declined from **215 households in 2022 (76%)** down to **194 households as of June 2026 (68%)**—a net loss of 21 contributing families.
* **Expanding Free-Rider Gap:** Over **31% of the community (90 homes)** currently does not contribute to essential maintenance, despite benefiting directly from shared greenspaces, tree care, entrance maintenance, and liability protections.
* **Annual Revenue Shortfall:** Proposed SCCA budgets consistently target **250 paying homes ($18,750)**. Under voluntary collection, annual revenue is trailing **$3,000 to $4,200 below planned operational capacity**.
* **The SCBD Solution:** Establishing an SCBD converts voluntary collections into a uniform annual assessment billed directly on Anne Arundel County property tax statements, ensuring **100% participation** and a guaranteed, predictable funding base.

---

## 2. Essential Overhead Costs Are Exploding

* **Insurance Costs Escalated Over 130%:** SCCA insurance premiums remained steady at ~$1,850 through 2024. However, premiums jumped to **$3,823 in 2025 (+102%)** and reached **$4,279 as of mid-2026**—already exceeding the entire 2026 annual insurance budget ($3,850) by mid-year.
* **Fixed Overhead Swallows Revenue:** Insurance alone now consumes nearly **30% of total collected dues**, leaving significantly fewer funds for actual physical neighborhood improvements.

---

## 3. High Volatility in Canopy & Common-Ground Liabilities

* **Record Tree Maintenance Expenses:** As community tree canopies age, hazard tree removal costs have spiked dramatically. Tree care expenses reached **$15,050 in 2025**—exceeding its $10,000 budget by **$5,050** and consuming over **70% of all collected community dues** for that year.
* **Inability to Plan Capital Reserve Upgrades:** Unpredictable tree removal emergencies combined with declining dues participation leave zero room for proactive infrastructure maintenance or long-term neighborhood enhancements.

---

## 4. Reserves Are Shrinking Toward Vulnerable Baselines

* **Operating Deficits:** SCCA ran a **$2,900.87 net operating deficit in 2025** ($20,895.87 actual spending vs. $17,995.00 budget) as cost spikes outpaced revenue collections.
* **Depleting Unencumbered Cash:** Net operating cash (excluding restricted funds such as playground reserves) dropped from **$13,166.16 at year-end 2024** to **$10,430.29 at year-end 2025**, and further down to **$6,763.21 by mid-2026**.
* **Fiscal Cushion Depleted:** Available cash is approaching 2020 levels ($6,640.60), leaving the community vulnerable to a single major storm event or emergency tree hazard.

---

## 5. Summary Arguments for SCBD Designation

* **Fairness & Equity:** Spreads the financial responsibility evenly across all 284 properties so that a shrinking group of paying neighbors no longer subsidizes the entire community's common maintenance.
* **Financial Stability & Tax Efficiency:** Provides stable funding for recurring liabilities (insurance, tree care, lawn care) while placing oversight and billing administration under standard county mechanisms.
* **Protecting Property Values:** Ensures common grounds, tree canopies, entrance signs, and neighborhood aesthetics are reliably maintained year-round without risking emergency assessments or degraded maintenance standards.
